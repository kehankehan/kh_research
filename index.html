01

Research programme

I investigate a single problem at different scales: how experience becomes structured in the moment, stabilised as concepts, and shared—or not—across minds.

Embodied cognition and emotion meet in this question. Both concern how bodily experience is organised into patterns that guide action, support judgement and become available for comparison.

01

Experience in action

Perceptual coordination

I examine how movement and expertise organise perception in natural environments, using synchronised eye tracking and inertial sensing to analyse gaze–head coordination in climbers.

  • Naturalistic bouldering
  • 26-climber dataset
  • Competition field sites

02

Experience in concepts

Representational geometry

I examine how emotion concepts are organised relationally, using human judgements and language-based methods to map structure rather than treating emotions as isolated labels.

  • 99 emotion terms
  • Human relational judgements
  • Cross-linguistic affective data

03

Structure across minds

Human–model alignment

I compare these structures across expertise, languages and cultures, then ask whether large language models recover the organisation found in human experience.

  • English · Chinese · Japanese
  • Representational comparison
  • Large language models

From question to evidence

I initiate questions, build the methods needed to test them, and carry projects through data collection, analysis and publication.

Selected evidence of independent research ownership

Climbing cognition

I built the field research pipeline.

I developed a synchronised head-mounted eye-tracking and inertial-sensing workflow, designed a naturalistic bouldering protocol and collected a 26-climber dataset. I also secured institutional ethics approval for research at national climbing competitions.

Manuscript under review · Open dataset in preparation

Emotion and AI

I developed a new human–model comparison.

I developed and analysed a method for mapping the relational structure of 99 emotion terms in humans and GPT-4, then led the work through publication. I am now extending that programme across English, Chinese and Japanese.

First-author article · Scientific Reports, 2026

Research infrastructure

I create systems that others can reuse.

Across appointments, I have led studies from design to publication, established an EEG laboratory, created an open multilingual speech-perception task and released documented datasets and preregistrations.

Open tasks · Data · Documented methods

Why language

Language makes conceptual structure accessible.

Having lived and worked in China, the United Kingdom, Singapore and Japan—and moving among Mandarin Chinese, Cantonese, English and Japanese—I encounter language as one of the systems through which people categorise and communicate experience.

My earlier research examined categorisation in bilingual speech perception. I now use cross-linguistic comparison to investigate how emotion concepts are structured across people, cultures and large language models.

04

How I investigate structure

Across laboratory experiments and natural movement, I identify patterns of relation and test what organises them.

Observe

Neurophysiology, behaviour, gaze and movement in laboratory and naturalistic settings.

Represent

Distance structures, time series and multidimensional spaces derived from human data.

Compare

Development, expertise, languages, cultures and biological or artificial systems.

Share

Preregistered studies, open datasets and reusable measurement and analysis tools.

05

A life in research

The questions I investigate grow from a life lived across languages, countries and communities.

I began formal research training in my thirties, after working in counselling and science communication. Since then, I have built a research career across the United Kingdom, Singapore and Japan. Taking this route taught me to enter new fields, learn their methods and choose questions for myself.

I am also a climber. Climbing is a practice I inhabit, a community I work with and a setting where questions about perception and action become visible. It has taken me from personal experience to instrumented research and scientific service in competitive climbing.

Through writing and the Chinese-language climbing podcast 一起爬V8 (Project V8), I bring research into conversation with wider publics. I am especially interested in how science can illuminate cognition, women’s lives and sport without flattening lived experience.
